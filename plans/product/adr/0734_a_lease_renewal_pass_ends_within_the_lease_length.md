# ADR 0734: a lease renewal pass ends within the lease length

Status: accepted for implementation, 2026-11-14. Not delivered; the plan stays off main until its reviewed implementation follows. Defect DC-237, which this narrows and does not close.

## Why
- A node's lease manager runs one renewal pass at a time: `run_lease_refresh_schedule` awaits a pass before it can take another timer or epoch wake. A pass reads the control leader once (`refresh_leases_once`) and then, range by range (Today) or batch by batch (ParentDecisions), either asks that leader (`request_remote_leases`, and Today's per-range request: connection, stream open, write, finish, read) or, when it is the leader, commits the grant itself (`client_write`). None of those waits has a deadline.
- So one request that gets no answer holds the pass, and with it every later pass: the node never re-reads who leads, while consensus and other proposals go on.
- Demonstrated in process on unchanged code (`lease1.log`, one execution): with every stream a follower opens to its leader held by the transport and its renewals seen to have stopped arriving, the leader was stopped and the other two agreed on a new one; the follower's own lease manager applied no renewal through the new leader within 15 s. The control, where the old leader's streams fail at once, renewed 0.56 s after the agreement. One failing case and one passing control, not a rate. What the test observes is a held route (every stream from that follower to that leader) and stopped renewals, not an intercepted lease request.
- Context, not attribution: two three-host serving-node-loss runs failed (at the default profile, and at a 250 ms consensus base, where one logical row was refused under three identities in the 18 s after the leader was killed). A byte screening of the second run's retained control logs is a screening only and establishes no absence of lease entries. Whether a held renewal was part of either run is not established; this ADR does not claim to make that scenario pass.

## Decision
1. **One budget per pass.** A renewal pass has one absolute deadline: its entry time plus the configured lease length (`ttl_ms`, 3 s at defaults). The renewal tick is unchanged and no setting is added. The lease length is the chosen patience, with its cost stated under Limits; it is not derived from any property of a grant. The deadline is applied when the pass is polled: it decides what the pass goes on waiting for and what it accepts. It cannot interrupt a stalled executor, blocking work or a future that does not yield, so "ends within the lease length" is a policy bound subject to scheduling, not a real-time guarantee, and no grace is added for that.
2. **Everything the pass waits on runs under it**, with no per-wait or per-chunk restart, and nothing is started once the budget is spent (the budget is checked before a wait is first polled, because polling can submit a request that discarding its result does not undo): the read of the current leader, the read of the applied targets, and for each chunk or range the remote request as one unit (connection, open, write, finish, read) or the local commit. No task is detached to enforce it.
3. **Expiry ends the pass.** The first wait that reaches the deadline ends the pass there: nothing further is attempted in it. The scheduler then starts the next pass at its next wake, which reads leadership again as it already does. No peer is avoided afterwards and no connection is evicted.
4. **A timed-out request is ambiguous**, neither a denial nor a cancellation: a dropped request can still be committed. Only a response that is whole, valid and OBSERVED before the deadline updates the local lease cache; a response that completes after it is discarded (the clock is read after the answer, before anything is cached). Leases cached from earlier chunks of the same pass, and from earlier passes, stay, under their own expiry and the existing gates. Nothing is cleared because a later chunk timed out.
5. **The next pass decides nothing from the timeout.** It derives its targets and expected generations from the applied state as now and stamps each request when it is built; whether a lost grant turns out to have applied (so the next is a renewal of it), lapsed (a regrant under a new generation) or is refused for a stale generation is the reducer's existing decision. Non-shortening and every authority check are unchanged.
6. **Fair continuation.** A pass that ends by budget leaves a cursor, a range start, on the data server: when a range or batch was ATTEMPTED and not answered after EARLIER units of the same pass had used part of the budget, that same unit (it has not had a whole budget yet, and the next pass begins with it); when it went unanswered as the pass's FIRST unit, the first target after that whole unit, wrapping to the walk's first when the unit was its last (it had a whole budget; the others are not made to wait behind it again, and when it is reached later and goes unanswered as a later unit it is first once more in the pass after); when the budget was already spent BEFORE a range or batch was attempted, that unattempted target itself (it is not skipped as though it had timed out); when the budget is spent before the walk can begin (reading the leader or the targets), the cursor it found is kept. A completed walk, or one with no targets, clears it. The next pass builds its targets fresh, in the same sorted order, and begins at the first target whose range start is at or after the cursor, wrapping to the front: each current target exactly once, cut into batches anew; no target list, batch or expected generation is carried from one pass to the next. The cursor is read and written whole under a short lock that is never held across a wait; passes are sequential in production, but the cursor is only advisory, so an overlapping direct call (tests make them) can at worst choose a different starting point. The cursor is a key, not an index, so ranges gained, lost or re-generated between passes need no bookkeeping: a cursor naming a range no longer held simply selects the next one. Consequences: a unit that keeps running out of budget behind others gets a whole budget in the next pass (two batches of 2 s under a 3 s budget are each answered in alternate passes, where "always continue after the unanswered unit" would never answer the second); a unit that never answers costs one whole pass in two and cannot keep the others from being attempted. No concurrency is added. ParentDecisions applies it to its batches (a batch is the unit: the cursor is the start of the next batch's first range); Today applies it to its ranges.
7. **Both modes.** The Today per-range path and the ParentDecisions batch path get the same budget, expiry and continuation rule; each keeps its own request, validation and cache code. No other request type is touched.
8. **Visible.** Budget expiry takes the existing error path once per pass (`data_ring_lease_errors_total`, the "renewal failed; will retry" warning) naming the peer or "local commit", the deadline, and how many targets were left unattempted; unattempted targets are not each logged.

## What does not change
Lease length, renewal tick, takeover grace, protection and stagger; grant admission and the lineage rules; what a write or read gate requires of a cached lease; the consensus timing profile; transport timeouts and connection reuse; the takeover and maintenance loops; any wire or durable format; any public setting.

## Limits, stated
- On a path where one renewal round trip plus its commit regularly exceeds the lease length, every pass ends by budget and the node caches no lease, where today a slow answer would eventually be cached (for a lease that had by then expired). The warning and the metric make that visible; it is not hidden by a longer wait.
- A pass can now stop part-way: with many batches and a slow leader a node's ranges are renewed over several passes. Continuation bounds how unfair that is; it does not make it fast.
- Re-reading leadership is delayed by up to one lease length after a request is first held, plus the tick.
- An unconfirmed reply supplies no NEW cached authority. An ambiguous grant can leave the cluster holding a lease for this node that the node does not know it has until its next successful pass; a lease the node had validly cached earlier still admits service until its own validity ends, and is not cleared; other nodes remain fenced by the applied lease.
- It does not bound any other loop's waits, and it does not show the three-host scenario recovers.

## Algebra
- A pass is a finite walk over an ordered target list from a start key; its result is (leases cached from in-deadline valid responses, optional cursor). Budget expiry changes where the walk stops and where the next begins, never what a response must satisfy to be cached.
- Cache writes: only `valid ∧ observed ≤ deadline`. The deadline can remove a cache write; it cannot add one or alter a cached lease.
- The cursor is advisory: any value, including a stale or absent one, yields a walk over exactly the current targets in rotated order.

## Acceptance
1. The held-route RED (`lease_renewal`), with two oracle corrections and its comments reworded to what it observes: the final poll reads the clock with each observation and accepts progress only when observed before the absolute 15 s mark; the fresh lease must be issued by the NEW leader, for the expected holder and range, with a start later than the baseline. It fails on unchanged code and passes with the change, the route fault unchanged. The responsive control still passes.
2. A grant that was applied with its answer withheld past the deadline, observed applied before anything can overwrite it: nothing is cached from that answer, the cache keeps what it had, and a later pass against current state is admitted as the reducer decides and is cached.
3. Leader side: a local commit that cannot complete (no quorum) ends the pass at the budget; the next pass proceeds.
4. Fair continuation, in small deterministic controls on the pass's own helpers: the walk holds each current target once from the cursor and wraps; expiry before an attempt leaves that target as the continuation; a unit unanswered behind earlier units is first in the next pass and is answered there (virtual time: two units of 2 s under a 3 s budget); a unit that never answers, first in the list, does not keep the others from being answered; a cursor naming a range not held, or none, is harmless; a completed pass clears it. Work is not polled once the budget is spent.
5. One focused Today control for the budget and the continuation.
6. Existing lease tests (`refresh_leases_once…`, the schedule tests, `primary_down`) pass unchanged.
Checks: focused selection of the lease, takeover and cluster modules; ONE whole-crate run after the implementation is stable; ONE reviewed three-host serving-node-loss execution at the unchanged 250 ms consensus base and 2000 ms query profile as the real-daemon record for a request-path change, reported whatever it does. No new profile, no cap change.
Cost: product delta under about 100 lines (network/data_server.rs, lease_batch.rs); tests about 300 lines; build about 8 minutes; focused tests minutes; whole crate about 13 minutes; the three-host run about 15 minutes.
